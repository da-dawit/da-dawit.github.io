July 11, 2026

Guest Instructor, “Learning AI through Self-Driving Robots”

Geumcheon Science Cube Talent-Sharing Program, Seoul

I taught 20 elementary students in grades 4 to 6 with Ubion’s DeepCo self-driving cars and training interface, through the whole pipeline: collecting driving data, preprocessing, training a LeNet-5 model, and running inference on the car. Along the way I covered what AI is and how models learn: neural networks, gradient descent, backpropagation, learning rate, and epochs.

Fall 2025 and Spring 2026

Teaching Assistant

Taejae University, Natural Sciences

I ran weekly sessions, graded problem sets and projects, and gave lectures linking AI and electronics to the math and physics beneath them.

NS200

Physics in the Modern Society, Fall 2025, San Francisco

  • Graded all problem sets: relative motion, dynamics with drag, rotational motion, thermodynamics, and DC circuit analysis.
  • Wrote extra thermodynamics worksheets beyond the course: the Clausius definition of entropy, the Brayton cycle behind jet engines, and compressible flow and shock waves past Mach 1.
  • Organized field trips to Stanford and UC Berkeley labs, the California Academy of Sciences, Intuitive (surgical robotics), and Synergy.
  • Ran the Natural Sciences Instagram and built the taejae-ns website for students’ work.

XD221

Principles of Quantitative Thinking in the Sciences, Spring 2026, New York

  • Ran the weekly worksheets and unit reviews on the Principles of Calculus text: sets, cardinality, and commutative diagrams; linear algebra from span and bases through orthogonality, determinants, linear transformations, rank and kernel, change of basis, and least squares; then sequences, continuity, big-O, and the chain rule behind backpropagation.
  • Prepared students for the oral final and advised their deep learning projects, from aerospace trajectories to finance.

July 2022 and July 2023, Phnom Penh, Cambodia

STEM Teachers Training

KOICA, the Korea Productivity Center, and the National Incubation Center of Cambodia

Two summers teaching Cambodian high-school science teachers to build and program robots, in “STEM Training Practice for Physics Using Robot and STEM Education” (July 4 to 8, 2022 and July 3 to 5, 2023).

2022

Arduino and a basketball robot

My presentation took Arduino from the ground up: the board as the robot’s brain, sensors as its senses, and motors as its muscles; how digital, analog, and PWM pins differ; and a first program that drives a robot around a chair.

Beyond the presentation, I helped the teachers wire all of their sensors and set up a controller to drive the robot around. Then we built a basketball robot that scoops up a ball and drops it into the basket, and I helped with the coding and wiring throughout. For their assignment, the teachers had to design the best scooper.

2023

A line-tracing sumo robot

I taught the firmware/hardware for a robot built on an Arduino Mega 2560, with an L298 motor driver, an ultrasonic sensor, and a three-part line sensor. In the first mission it follows a course and counts the cross lines to know when to take a curve and where to stop. In the second it fights: it searches for the other robot, pushes when it is close, and backs away when its line sensor sees the white edge of the ring. I also covered the wiring mistakes that stop a robot most often: a common ground for the Arduino and the motor driver, and a separate battery for the motors so a failing motor cannot take the controller down with it. The week ended with the teachers’ robots competing on the course and in the ring.

March to summer 2023

Amphibian conservation, Gangnam-gu

Night surveys of salamanders in Gangnam-gu’s urban wetlands during the spawning season: counting egg masses and adults at each site and tracking how the numbers changed from survey to survey. With local environmental groups and the Korea Amphibian and Reptile Conservation Network, we turned those counts into posters and street campaigns, and into proposals to the local government for protecting the habitat.

Geumcheon science programs

Geumcheon’s science programs are where I built my first robots, and where I went back to help younger students build theirs. I mentored the district’s elementary team for the World Robot Olympiad in Korea, 28 students in 7 teams over 10 weeks, teaching them the algorithms and how to design and build their robots, and ran maker workshops at the district’s science festival.

October 2021 to January 2022

A smart farm, built with robots and code

The last stage of Geumcheon’s three-stage hackathon training with Dongyang Mirae University. After training on Jimu robots and building services for the Alpha Mini humanoid, my teammates and I built a working smart-farm model, run by robots and code, and finished it in January 2022.
