Who is Dawit Chun?

I’m a senior at Taejae University in Seoul, applying to robotics PhD programs for fall 2027. Have you ever played bingo? I’d like to introduce myself with one. Each tile is a value that shaped my life and my wish to become a robotics engineer. Five in a line make a bingo.

Courage

Before university, I left the usual path through school to study for my own dream. People around me worried, and some said it out loud: “What can a kid even do?” and “Isn’t he just a guinea pig?” But I had a pioneer’s vision. Because it was my own choice, I had to carry its weight, like running a marathon. At 15, I entered Taejae University, Korea’s first innovative university, as the youngest student in its first cohort, with the highest entrance score. Taejae’s students move to cities around the world and take on local problems as civic projects. Taejae took a brave first step toward the future of education, and I knocked on its door. So I fill my first tile with Courage.

Challenge

Through countless challenges and failures, I have proved what I can do: a robot contest grand prize (2021), the KSME Gold Prize (2022), gold at the Aramco Coding World Cup (2023), gold at the International Robot Olympiad in Athens (2024), 1st of 84 at the ACM CHI 2025 Student Design Competition (2025), and a first-author paper at IEEE ICCD (2026). Each award came from a real problem: people trapped in semi-basement homes during monsoon floods, energy, and elderly people with dementia who wander. So I fill my second tile with Challenge.

Innovation

To me, first place at the International Robot Olympiad did not mean being called the best. What the win gave me was a clear idea of what to do next. At Ubion, I helped build new AI learning kits, the DeepCo self-driving car and the DeepCo Board, so that more students in Korea could get close to AI and go on to world competitions themselves. So I fill this tile with Innovation.

Responsibility

I tried to let go of a narrow, selfish view and remember the wider world. Through the Oxford-Stanford-Taejae Fellowship, I went to Oxford as one of Korea’s representatives in a debate on the UN Sustainable Development Goals, and in August 2025 my teammate and I won the AI policy debate in Seoul against students from Oxford and Stanford, then presented our recommendations to Korea’s Deputy Prime Minister. I wrote about these experiences in two chapters of a book published with Korea’s Office for Government Policy Coordination. So I fill this tile with Responsibility.

Sharing

Thinking about it, what good is it to keep everything I had seen outside Korea, and outside my own small world, to myself? Why not share it back? We gave our whole $2,000 debate prize to WWF Korea. I taught about 30 science teachers in Cambodia over two summers with KOICA, and now robots are taught in classrooms across Cambodia. Since 2021, I have kept Korean sponsors in touch with the children they support through U9 Change. I gave my Talent Award of Korea prize to a scholarship foundation in Geumcheon. And very recently, I didn’t want it to end there, so I taught 20 elementary students in Geumcheon how to train a self-driving car’s AI, the very thing I won my gold medal for. So I fill the last tile of my first line with Sharing.

Technology

At ROBOTIS in summer 2026, I built the teleoperation system for HG-DAgger, a form of human-in-the-loop learning: 31 demonstrations and one round of 13 corrections taught the AI Worker a long peg-and-screw task. The same sim-to-real off-policy RL raised Prior-ACT, which I built, from 60% to 82%, and TurboVLA only from 17% to about 30%.

Compassion

Between 173,000 and 232,500 people with ALS cannot speak. I built iPhoneme’s decoder, which reads a 256-electrode speech implant at 92.14% phoneme accuracy on the T15 dataset, about 3 points above the previous best.

A bigger bingo

I expect to graduate in June 2027. At ROBOTIS, TurboVLA succeeded only with bottles within 1 to 2 cm of the table’s center, and I think the limit was its base representation. As a PhD student in your lab, I want to work on robot manipulation with vision-language-action and world models, and to be part of taking the next step toward AGI. My goal is to become a robot engineer who saves people.
